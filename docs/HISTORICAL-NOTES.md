# Historical notes

## Arabic historical names

Arabic localization uses Modern Standard Arabic, with Tunisian conventions preferred for certain
historical names and transcriptions, particularly those relating to Carthage and ancient North
Africa. The reviewed project glossary and source notes are maintained in
[`LOCALIZATION-AR-TN.md`](LOCALIZATION-AR-TN.md). Internal content IDs remain language-independent.

Pax Mediterranea simplifies history for clarity and balance. Factions are abstractions; borders and
dates vary by scenario; leaders and forces may be combined outside exact chronology. Divine favors
represent belief, morale, ritual, and political legitimacy, not proven supernatural events.
Uncertainty should be marked with language such as “historical sources suggest,” “this scenario
simplifies,” and “the exact details remain debated.”

Rome is not the default protagonist. Carthage is treated as a complex Phoenician-founded North
African and Mediterranean civilization with civic, commercial, maritime, religious, and military
life—not merely Rome's enemy. Use “Carthaginian,” “Punic” where appropriate, “Hamilcar Barca,” and
“Baal Hammon.”

Starting references for human review:

- Dexter Hoyos, _The Carthaginians_ (Routledge, 2010).
- Richard Miles, _Carthage Must Be Destroyed_ (Allen Lane, 2010).
- J. F. Lazenby, _The First Punic War_ (Stanford University Press, 1996).
- Polybius, _Histories_, consulted as an ancient literary source with authorial and transmission caveats.
- British Museum and Metropolitan Museum of Art collection essays for material-culture context.

Summaries must be original; these references are not permission to copy text.
